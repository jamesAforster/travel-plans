Option A: Colombia + Peru (Recommended)

~10 days Colombia, ~18 days Peru. Two countries, one continent, no backtracking.

Route: London → Bogotá → (bus/fly to) Cartagena → Caribbean coast → fly Bogotá → Lima → Cusco/Sacred Valley → Amazon → fly Lima → London

Flights: ~£800–900 per person (London→Bogotá one-way ~£375, Bogotá→Lima ~£175, Lima→London ~£350–400)

Daily budget (per person, private double room): £25–45/day. Private rooms in guesthouses £8–15/night each. Street food and local restaurants £5–10/day. Local transport £3–5/day.

Estimated total per person: £1,550–2,250 including flights.

Country detail: Colombia and Peru (highlights, lowlights, June weather, people, language, costs).

Ecuador add-on: Ecuador slots between Colombia and Peru for just ~£50–80 extra in flights (Bogotá→Quito 1hr 40min, ~£100; Quito→Lima 2hrs, ~£100). Route becomes: London → Bogotá → Quito → Lima → London. See Ecuador; the Galápagos (£1,800+ for a cruise) is the one thing that blows the budget, so save it for another trip.

Option B: Peru Deep Dive (Cheapest)

A full month in one country means no rush, no extra flights, and time to really sink into places.

Route: London → Lima → Arequipa → Colca Canyon → Puno/Lake Titicaca → Cusco → Sacred Valley → Amazon → Huaraz (Cordillera Blanca) → Lima → London

Flights: ~£650 return London↔Lima.

Daily budget (per person, private double room): £30–50/day. Peru is very affordable outside Lima's tourist restaurants.

Estimated total per person: £1,570–2,200 including flights.

Country detail: Peru (highlights, lowlights, treks, June weather, people, language, costs).

What a month buys you: Time to trek the Ausangate circuit (5 days, almost nobody else on the trail), linger in Sacred Valley villages, spend 3–4 days in the Amazon, explore the Cordillera Blanca (some of the most dramatic mountain scenery in the world), visit Lake Titicaca's floating islands, eat your way through Lima, and still have rest days built in.

The trade-off: Less variety than two countries. Peru is extraordinary, but you miss Colombia's Caribbean coast, jungle treks, and coffee country. Peru is also very busy this time of year, so some trails will be packed.

Option C: Colombia + Peru + Buenos Aires (Most Ambitious)

Three countries, ending with a few days in one of the world's great cities. Skip Patagonia (see below), enjoy Buenos Aires itself.

Route: London → Bogotá → Cartagena → fly Bogotá → Lima → Cusco → Sacred Valley → fly Lima → Buenos Aires → London

Flights: ~£1,000–1,200 per person (London→Bogotá ~£375, Bogotá→Lima ~£175, Lima→Buenos Aires ~£140, Buenos Aires→London ~£450)

Daily budget (per person): £25–40/day in Colombia and Peru, £35–50/day in Buenos Aires.

Estimated total per person: £2,000–2,850 including flights.

Country detail: Argentina (Buenos Aires highlights, lowlights, weather, people, costs).

The trade-off: More time on planes, less time in each place. You'd have roughly 8 days Colombia, 14 days Peru, 5 days Buenos Aires. The extra flights add £200–300 per person over Option A.

Option D: SE Asia Islands — Borneo → Flores → Bali

The dry-season route: Borneo, Flores and Bali are all dry in June, and three stops keep the flights and transfer days down. The Philippines is left out because June is the start of its wet and typhoon season, and there is no direct flight from Cebu to Kota Kinabalu. Option G is the version with the Philippines added.

Route: London → Kota Kinabalu → Sepilok and the Kinabatangan River → fly to Labuan Bajo (via Kuala Lumpur or another hub) → Komodo and Flores → fly to Bali → Ubud and the coast → London

Time split: roughly 9 days in Borneo, 9 in Flores and Komodo, and 8 in Bali, including a travel day at each change.

Flights: ~£700–1,100 per person. London → Kota Kinabalu and home from Bali is about £550–850 (KK returns are quoted at £457–778 on Expedia and €549–622 on Skyscanner; Bali returns €470–800 on Momondo). Kota Kinabalu → Labuan Bajo is a one-stop trip, about £100–170 (Kiwi from £98). Labuan Bajo → Bali is about £50–70 (Expedia). None of these are quotes for June 2027.

Daily budget (per person, private double room): £25–40/day. Indonesia is very cheap (£20–35/day), Borneo slightly more (£30–45/day).

Estimated total per person: £1,400–2,200 including flights, excluding Komodo boat trips and Sipadan diving (add roughly £250–600 for those).

Country detail: Borneo, Flores and Komodo and Bali (highlights, lowlights, June weather, people, language, costs). The Philippines was dropped from this route.

The trade-off: three stops and three or four flights instead of five or six, but Kota Kinabalu → Labuan Bajo is still a long one-stop travel day. All three stops are dry in June. Less history and ancient civilisation than South America, and no longer the cheapest option. Komodo boat trips and Sipadan diving cost extra, and June is peak season there, so book 3–4 months ahead.

Option E: SE Asia Overland — Vietnam + Cambodia

More hops than any other option, but travel in SE Asia is so cheap that it barely moves the budget. North to south through Vietnam, then Angkor.

Route: London → Hanoi → Ha Giang Loop / Sapa → Ninh Binh → Hue → Hoi An → Ho Chi Minh City → Mekong Delta → Siem Reap → London

Time split: roughly 8 days in northern Vietnam, 6 in the centre, 5 in the south, and 8 in Cambodia.

Flights: ~£800–1,300 per person. London → Hanoi returns are quoted at about £400–690 on Expedia and Kayak. Flying home from Siem Reap is the expensive part: there are no direct flights to London, and returns are quoted from £870 on Vietnam Airlines, so a one-way home is roughly £450–650 (my estimate). Ho Chi Minh City → Siem Reap is about £25–45 by bus via Phnom Penh or £70–230 by air (guide).

Daily budget (per person, private double room): £20–35/day. Private rooms £8–15/night.

Estimated total per person: £1,400–2,300 including flights.

Country detail: Vietnam and Cambodia (highlights, lowlights, June weather, people, language, costs).

The trade-off: a new base every 3–4 days means less depth. But transport is so cheap (buses, sleeper trains, £30–60 domestic flights) that the extra travel hardly matters. June is hot, humid and showery, and there's less high-mountain drama than Peru.

Option F: The Caucasus — Georgia + Armenia (Cheapest)

A short flight, cool mountains, ancient monasteries and some of the most hospitable people anywhere, for the lowest total of any option.

Route: London → Tbilisi → Kazbegi → Kutaisi → Svaneti (Mestia, Ushguli) → Tbilisi → overland to Yerevan → Dilijan / Lake Sevan → Tatev → Yerevan → London

Time split: about two weeks in each country.

Flights: ~£250–500 per person, flying into Tbilisi and home from Yerevan. easyJet flies nonstop from London (about 5h20) and Wizz Air flies direct to Yerevan (about 5h15); Air France quotes Yerevan returns from £420. I couldn't find a Tbilisi → Yerevan minibus fare, but it's a short, cheap overland trip. Fares are snapshots, not June 2027 quotes.

Daily budget (per person, private double room): £20–40/day, with Armenia slightly cheaper than Georgia.

Estimated total per person: £800–1,600 including flights.

Country detail: Georgia and Armenia (highlights, lowlights, June weather, people, language, costs).

The trade-off: cheapest and closest, with great food, wine and hospitality. But it's less wild than South America or Borneo: no jungle and no big wildlife. High mountain trails in Svaneti and Kazbegi may still be snowed in during early June.

Option G: SE Asia Grand Tour — Philippines → Borneo → Flores → Bali

The original route, reworked: start in the Philippines in early June, when the weather is least risky, then fly south into Indonesia's dry season. Four stops make it the most travel of the SE Asia options, but you get the warmest people of any option.

Route: London → Manila → Cebu → Bohol → back to Cebu → fly via Manila to Kota Kinabalu → Sepilok and the Kinabatangan River → fly to Labuan Bajo (via Kuala Lumpur or another hub) → Komodo and Flores → fly to Bali → London

Time split: about a week in each of the four stops, including a travel day at each change.

Flights: ~£800–1,300 per person. London → Manila and home from Bali is about £550–850 (Manila returns are quoted from £384–404 and typically £667–838 on Kayak and Momondo; Bali returns €470–800 on Momondo). Manila → Cebu is about £25–60 (Expedia). Cebu → Manila → Kota Kinabalu is about £70–150: Manila to Kota Kinabalu is direct on AirAsia, from about £20 on Trip.com, which I'd treat as a floor, and there is no direct Cebu to Kota Kinabalu flight. Kota Kinabalu → Labuan Bajo is about £100–170 (Kiwi) and Labuan Bajo → Bali about £50–70 (Expedia). None of these are quotes for June 2027.

Daily budget (per person, private double room): £25–40/day. Philippines £25–35/day, Indonesia £20–35/day, Borneo £30–45/day.

Estimated total per person: £1,500–2,400 including flights, excluding Komodo boat trips and Sipadan diving (add roughly £250–600 for those).

Country detail: Philippines, Borneo, Flores and Komodo and Bali (highlights, lowlights, June weather, people, language, costs).

The trade-off: the most flights of any option, about eight including connections, and more transfer days, so less time in each place. June is the start of the Philippines' wet and typhoon season, so build in slack days at the start, and there is no direct Cebu to Kota Kinabalu flight. In return you get the warmest welcome of any option plus beaches, diving and wildlife. Less history and ancient civilisation than South America.

Colombia

The warm, social half of Option A: about 10 days of Caribbean coast, jungle and coffee country.

Highlights

  • Lost City trek: 4-day jungle trek to ancient ruins, far less touristy than Machu Picchu
  • Tayrona National Park: jungle meets Caribbean beach
  • Cartagena: the old walled city
  • Coffee region: stay on a working finca
  • Cocora Valley: towering wax palms
  • Medellín: street art and culture

Lowlights

  • Petty crime and scams. Pickpocketing in Bogotá, Medellín and Cartagena. Drink spiking and dating-app scams are a known problem in Medellín and Bogotá. Use taxi apps after dark.
  • Some areas are off limits. Parts of the Venezuelan border and a few rural regions still have armed groups. Check the FCDO advice and stick to well-travelled routes.
  • Cartagena's tourist zone is hot, humid and full of hustle.
  • Overland is slow. Winding mountain roads mean long bus days, so internal flights (£50–100) are often the better use of time.
  • Lost City is hard going. Hot, muddy and tiring, and only run as guided group treks.
  • English is rare outside the big cities.

June weather: afternoon showers but mornings clear, 20–30°C depending on altitude.

People & culture: effusively friendly. Very social, tactile and expressive, with real pride in showing visitors their country. Smaller towns and the coffee region are especially generous. The Cartagena tourist zone has more of a hustle.

Language: James speaks some Spanish, which will be a big advantage. Even basic conversational Spanish transforms the experience: people open up, you get invited to things, you eat where locals eat. Duolingo's Latin American Spanish course for a few months before departure would be ideal.

Costs: £25–45 per person per day with a private double room (the Option A figure for Colombia and Peru together). Guesthouse private rooms £8–15/night, street food and local restaurants £5–10/day, local transport £3–5/day.

Safety: vastly improved, but research specific areas. Stick to well-travelled routes and you'll be fine.

Peru

The longest leg of Option A (about 18 days) and the whole of Option B.

Highlights

  • Sacred Valley villages
  • Machu Picchu: day visit by train, skipping the Inca Trail
  • Colca Canyon: twice the depth of the Grand Canyon, condors overhead
  • Amazon rainforest from Puerto Maldonado
  • Lima: world-class food scene
  • Cusco: colonial and Inca architecture
  • With a full month (Option B): the Ausangate circuit (5 days, almost nobody else on the trail), Lake Titicaca's floating islands, and the Cordillera Blanca near Huaraz

Lowlights

  • Altitude sickness is real. Cusco is 3,400m. Plan 2–3 acclimatisation days before anything strenuous. Arequipa (2,300m) is a gentler starting point.
  • June is peak season. Machu Picchu, Cusco and the main trails are busy, and tickets for popular time slots sell out weeks ahead.
  • Cold nights. Near 0°C at altitude, and cheap guesthouses often have little or no heating. Check for heating or extra blankets when booking private rooms.
  • Lima is grey and damp in June, with overcast skies and drizzle at around 15–19°C. The food makes up for it.
  • Machu Picchu is the pricey bit. The train from Ollantaytambo is £20–50 each way, plus entry.
  • Long travel days. Big distances and winding mountain roads, so flights are often worth it.
  • Theft and disruption. Bag theft in Lima and Cusco, and occasional strikes and road blockades in the south. Check before moving on.

June weather: dry season in the Andes, with clear skies, 15–20°C days and cold nights near 0°C at altitude.

People & culture: rewards your effort. Lima is cosmopolitan and reserved. Andean communities are dignified and quieter: show genuine interest and people open up. Amazon communities are more open and curious.

Language: Spanish, as in Colombia. Outside major cities English is rare.

Costs: £25–45 per person per day on the Colombia + Peru route, £30–50 per day for a full month in Peru alone. Very affordable outside Lima's tourist restaurants.

Ecuador

An optional add-on between Colombia and Peru: 5–7 days for roughly £50–80 extra in flights compared with flying Bogotá → Lima direct.

Highlights

  • Quito: colonial old town
  • Cotopaxi volcano
  • Mindo: cloud forest
  • Otavalo: indigenous market
  • Affordable Amazon lodges
  • Route: London → Bogotá → Quito → Lima → London (Bogotá → Quito 1h40, about £100; Quito → Lima 2h, about £100)

Lowlights

  • Security has worsened. Gang violence has risen since 2023–24, with states of emergency declared. Some areas, especially around Guayaquil, the coast and the Colombian border, have FCDO warnings. Check before booking.
  • Petty crime in Quito, particularly in the old town at night.
  • Altitude. Quito is about 2,850m, so take the first day or two gently.
  • Cloud can hide the volcanoes. Cotopaxi views are never guaranteed.
  • 5–7 days is rushed, and it takes days from Colombia and Peru.
  • The Galápagos blow the budget. A cruise is £1,800+, so save it for another trip.

June weather: highlands are in their drier season: mild days, cool nights, and cloud on the volcano summits (approximate).

People & culture: friendly and patient. Similar to Peru but slightly warmer. Indigenous markets have a strong tradition of welcoming trade and interaction.

Language: Spanish, as in Colombia and Peru. English is rare outside major cities.

Costs: same daily budget as Colombia and Peru, about £25–45 per person per day.

Argentina

Only in Option C, as 4–5 days in Buenos Aires at the end of the trip.

Highlights

  • Steak: world-class, for £5–8
  • Tango shows and milongas
  • La Boca: street art
  • Recoleta: architecture and cemetery
  • Wine and vibrant nightlife
  • A great way to end the trip

Lowlights

  • It's all city. Buenos Aires goes against the brief of not spending all your time in cities.
  • More flights, more cost. Option C costs roughly £450–600 more per person than Option A in total (about £200–300 of that is flights), and the flight home is about £450.
  • June is winter. Cool and damp, around 8–16°C.
  • Pricier on the ground. £35–50/day versus £25–40 in Colombia and Peru.
  • Money is complicated. Exchange rates and the best way to pay have varied a lot, so check the current advice before going.
  • Late hours. Dinner often starts at 9–10pm.
  • Phone snatching and pickpocketing in the centre.
  • No Patagonia in June. See Why Not Patagonia in June.

June weather: southern-hemisphere winter, cool and damp (approximate).

People & culture: passionate and direct. The Vienna parallel: intense, opinionated, fun, with a European-influenced directness. Warm once you're in, but not instant. Small-town Argentina is softer.

Language: Spanish, with its own accent and the vos form, which takes a little getting used to.

Costs: £35–50 per person per day.

Philippines

The opening stop of Option G (about a week in Cebu and Bohol). Left out of Option D because June is the start of the wet and typhoon season, and there is no direct flight from Cebu to Kota Kinabalu, so you have to go back through Manila.

Highlights

  • Whale sharks
  • Chocolate Hills on Bohol
  • Island-hopping around Cebu and Bohol

Lowlights

  • June is the start of the rainy and typhoon season. Showers are manageable, but storms can cancel ferries and flights, so build in slack days.
  • Hot and humid, around 28–33°C.
  • Manila is heavy going. Traffic, pollution and crowds.
  • Oslob whale-shark tourism is ethically contested because the sharks are fed to draw them in. Worth researching an alternative operator.
  • Slow inter-island travel. Ferries and domestic flights eat days.
  • Less history and ancient civilisation than South America.

June weather: monsoon just arriving: manageable, with some showers.

People & culture: extraordinarily warm. Strangers invite you to eat with their family, and people walk you to where you're going rather than point. Almost overwhelming hospitality.

Language: Filipinos mostly speak good English, so this is the lowest language barrier of all the countries. A salamat (thank you) still delights people.

Costs: cheap, £25–35 per person per day with a private double room. London → Manila is about £300.

Borneo

Sabah, Malaysia: the first stop of Option D (about 9 days) and the second of Option G (about a week), for wildlife and diving.

Highlights

  • Orangutans at Sepilok
  • Kinabatangan river safari
  • Sipadan diving

Lowlights

  • Eastern Sabah security advisories. The Semporna area, where Sipadan diving is based, has had FCDO restrictions in the past. Check the current advice before booking.
  • Sipadan is limited and pricey. Daily dive permits are capped and need booking ahead, and you must be a certified diver.
  • Wildlife is never guaranteed. Orangutan and river sightings vary.
  • Hot and humid, with showers even in the drier months.
  • Another flight to fit in, and little in the way of history or art.

June weather: dry season.

People & culture: laid-back and easy. Multicultural and helpful without being overbearing. Indigenous communities are hospitable and curious.

Language: English is widely spoken in Malaysian Borneo.

Costs: slightly higher than its neighbours, £30–45 per person per day.

Flores and Komodo

Indonesia, the second stop of Option D (about 9 days) and the third of Option G (about a week), around Labuan Bajo and the Komodo islands.

Highlights

  • Komodo dragons
  • Pink beach and snorkelling, with some of the clearest underwater visibility of the year in June
  • Volcanic lakes on Flores, such as Kelimutu's coloured crater lakes (a long trip east of Labuan Bajo)
  • Very few tourists once you leave the Labuan Bajo boat-trip hub

Lowlights

  • June is peak season. Boat trips and liveaboards cost more and sell out, so book dive and boat trips 3–4 months ahead (Dive Tribe Komodo).
  • Labuan Bajo is a busy boat-trip hub, so Komodo trips can be crowded.
  • Flores is hard going. Long days on rough, winding roads, basic facilities and few ATMs outside the towns.
  • Boat trips depend on the seas, and schedules shift.
  • Stomach upsets are common.

June weather: dry season with calm seas, roughly 23–31°C. One source lists some June rainfall, but most describe it as very dry.

People & culture: quietly warm. Flores and the eastern islands are special: very few tourists, genuine curiosity and an unperformative welcome.

Language: Bahasa Indonesia, as in Bali. English is fine in the Labuan Bajo tourist area but rare in villages.

Costs: very cheap, £20–35 per person per day with a private double room, though June boat trips and diving cost extra.

Bali

Indonesia, the final stop of Option D (about 8 days) and Option G (about a week): a wind-down after Flores.

Highlights

  • Ubud: rice terraces and temples
  • Mount Batur at sunrise
  • Beaches to finish on

Lowlights

  • Peak dry season. Busy and traffic-clogged, and Kuta and Seminyak are more transactional.
  • Scooter accidents are a common way for holidays to go wrong. Think twice before renting one.
  • Stomach upsets (Bali belly) are common.
  • Less history and ancient civilisation than South America.

June weather: peak dry season, 27–31°C.

People & culture: gentle, gracious and spiritual. Touristy Bali (Kuta) is more transactional; Ubud and rural Bali feel warmer.

Language: learn basic Bahasa Indonesia. It's one of the simplest languages to pick up, with no verb conjugation and no gendered nouns. Terima kasih (thank you), berapa? (how much?) and permisi (excuse me) go a long way.

Costs: very cheap, £20–35 per person per day with a private double room.

Vietnam

The main stretch of Option E: about three weeks from Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City.

Highlights

  • Ha Giang Loop: limestone mountain passes and minority villages in the far north
  • Sapa: rice terraces and village treks
  • Ninh Binh and Lan Ha Bay: karst scenery by boat and bike
  • Hue: imperial citadel and royal tombs
  • Hoi An: old town and the Hai Van Pass
  • Phong Nha: huge caves in the central highlands
  • Mekong Delta: floating markets and canal villages
  • The food: among the best street food anywhere

Lowlights

From general knowledge, not yet checked against current sources.

  • Hot and humid. Around 30–35°C in June, with thunderstorms most afternoons in the north and south.
  • Rainy season is starting in the north and south. The central coast is the driest, hottest stretch.
  • Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City traffic is intense, and scooters rule the roads.
  • Tourist hotspots are crowded and hustly: Ha Long Bay cruises, Sapa and Hoi An.
  • Ha Giang Loop accidents. Most people ride pillion with a local driver, which is the safer way.
  • Long bus and sleeper-train days, though they are very cheap.
  • Bag snatching and overcharging in the big cities.

June weather: hot and humid, with the heaviest rain in the north and south and the central coast hottest and driest (approximate).

People & culture: warm, curious and cheerful, though touts are common in tourist areas.

Language: Vietnamese is tonal and hard, but English is common in tourist areas. Cảm ơn (thank you) is appreciated.

Costs: cheap, about £20–35 per person per day with a private room at £8–15/night. Buses, sleeper trains and domestic flights (roughly £30–60) make the long distances affordable.

Cambodia

The final week of Option E: Siem Reap and Angkor, with Phnom Penh and Kampot as options.

Highlights

  • Angkor Wat, Bayon and Ta Prohm: the world's largest religious monument complex and jungle-wrapped temples
  • Tonle Sap: floating villages
  • Phnom Penh: Royal Palace and the sobering history of S-21 and the Killing Fields
  • Kampot and Kep: riverside slow living, pepper farms and crab
  • Ratanakiri in the far northeast: jungle, waterfalls and very few visitors

Lowlights

From general knowledge, not yet checked against current sources.

  • June is the start of the rainy season. Hot, humid and around 30–35°C, with afternoon downpours. It's also green and quieter.
  • Angkor heat. Sunrise starts are early, and the middle of the day is punishing.
  • Siem Reap is very tourist-focused, with touts and overpriced temple extras.
  • Phnom Penh sites are heavy going, and motorbike bag snatching happens.
  • Landmines remain in remote areas. Stay on marked paths.
  • Slow roads and buses, and limited rail.

June weather: hot, humid, with afternoon showers (approximate).

People & culture: gentle, resilient and welcoming.

Language: Khmer. English is common in Siem Reap and Phnom Penh. US dollars are widely used for payment.

Costs: about £20–30 per person per day with a private room. An Angkor pass is roughly US$37 for one day (approximate).

Georgia

The first half of Option F: about two weeks of mountains, monasteries and wine.

Highlights

  • Kazbegi: Gergeti Trinity Church beneath Mount Kazbek
  • Svaneti: Mestia and Ushguli's medieval stone towers, with multi-day hiking between them
  • Tbilisi: old town, sulphur baths and a great food scene
  • Kutaisi: Martvili and Okatse canyons
  • Vardzia and Uplistsikhe: cave monastery and ancient cave town
  • Kakheti: wine country and the world's oldest winemaking tradition
  • Food: khachapuri, khinkali and long feasts

Lowlights

From general knowledge, not yet checked against current sources.

  • High passes can still be snowbound in June. Some Svaneti and Kazbegi trails and roads may be closed or muddy until later in the month.
  • Hot lowlands. Tbilisi can reach the 30s in summer.
  • Marshrutka minibuses are cramped, fast and rough on mountain roads.
  • Occupied regions are off limits. The FCDO advises against travel to Abkhazia and South Ossetia.
  • Political protests have affected Tbilisi in recent years, so check current advice.
  • Nature is less wild than South America: no jungle and no big wildlife.

June weather: warm in the lowlands and cooler in the mountains. Georgia is hotter than Armenia in summer, and the alpine hiking season is just opening (30 Sundays).

People & culture: hospitality is legendary. Guests are treated as a gift, and invitations to eat and drink come easily.

Language: Georgian has its own script, which is hard. English is spoken by younger people in Tbilisi, Russian by many older people. Madloba is thank you.

Costs: about £25–40 per person per day with a private room. Cash is wise in villages.

Armenia

The second half of Option F: about two weeks of monasteries, mountains and history.

Highlights

  • Yerevan: the Cascade, Matenadaran manuscripts and cafe culture
  • Garni and Geghard: a Greek-style temple and a monastery carved into a cliff
  • Khor Virap: the classic view of Mount Ararat
  • Tatev Monastery: reached by the Wings of Tatev cable car
  • Dilijan and Lake Sevan: forest hiking and a high mountain lake
  • Haghpat and Sanahin: UNESCO monasteries in the north
  • Areni: one of the oldest wine regions in the world

Lowlights

From general knowledge, not yet checked against current sources.

  • Borders with Azerbaijan and Turkey are closed, and areas near the Azerbaijan border can be tense. Check the FCDO advice.
  • Changeable June weather. Yerevan is warm, around 28°C, but June is changeable with around 10 rainy days (Avionero).
  • Hot Ararat valley in the middle of the day.
  • English is limited outside Yerevan, and Russian is widely understood.
  • Public transport is by minibus, and hiking infrastructure is less developed than in Georgia.

June weather: warm and changeable, with rain possible (see above).

People & culture: warm and generous, with similar hospitality to Georgia.

Language: Armenian has its own alphabet. English is fine in Yerevan, and Russian helps elsewhere.

Costs: slightly cheaper than Georgia, especially food (30 Sundays). About £20–35 per person per day with a private room.

Why Not Patagonia in June

June is deep winter in the southern hemisphere. Patagonia in June means:

  • Temperatures of -10°C to 0°C, heavy snowfall, ~8 hours of daylight
  • Torres del Paine: the O Circuit is closed, the W Trek is closed (June–July), most refugios and campsites shut down
  • Most trails, restaurants, and accommodations close for the season
  • Perito Moreno Glacier remains open year-round but with reduced hours (9am–4pm) and fewer tours

Patagonia in winter is beautiful from a car window or a hotel lobby, but the trekking adventure you're imagining isn't available. The classic Patagonia trip — hiking Torres del Paine, El Chaltén, glaciers — needs December to March.

The recommendation: Save Patagonia for a dedicated trip in southern summer (Dec–Feb). It deserves its own week or two when you can actually hike it.

Key Tips

  • Book flights 9–10 months ahead (Aug–Sep 2026) for the best prices. Set up Skyscanner price alerts now.
  • Altitude sickness is real. Cusco is 3,400m. Plan 2–3 acclimatisation days before doing anything strenuous. Arequipa (2,300m) is a gentler starting point.
  • Book Machu Picchu tickets early. June is peak season — tickets for popular time slots sell out weeks ahead.
  • Travel insurance is essential. Make sure it covers trekking at altitude and emergency evacuation.
  • Brush up your Spanish. Even basics go a long way outside tourist hubs. Neither Colombia nor Peru is particularly English-speaking outside major cities.
  • Colombia safety: Vastly improved, but research specific areas. Stick to well-travelled routes and you'll be fine.
  • Private rooms on a budget: Guesthouses and hostels with private doubles are plentiful in both countries, typically £8–20/night for two.
